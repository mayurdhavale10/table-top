# Table Top: Content Strategy & Copywriting

This document contains the finalized marketing copy used across the Table Top landing page and pitch decks. The tone should always be direct, professional, and slightly disruptive, calling out industry standards as outdated.

## 1. The Hero Section (The Hook)
**Badge:** The Boutique Restaurant Tech
**Headline:** Stop handing your customers a 2010 PDF.
**Sub-headline:** Table Top is the premium, zero-install digital menu that looks as good as your food tastes. No expensive ERPs. No clunky hardware. Just one QR code.
**Primary CTA:** View Live Menu
**Secondary CTA:** Try Cafe Dashboard

## 2. The Broken Experience (Pain Points)
These sections highlight the exact friction points experienced by the two key demographics: the Restaurant Owner and the Diner.

**Card 1 (For Cafes): The Reprint Nightmare**
> Prices doubled overnight? Marking an item 'Sold Out' or changing a price shouldn't require a developer or reprinting 50 physical menus.

**Card 2 (For Diners): The Fake Price Tag**
> You never know the real menu or the real prices because third-party delivery apps inflate them by 30% to cover commissions.

**Card 3 (For Cafes): Expensive Hardware**
> Why pay ₹50,000 for clunky ordering tablets and rigid ERPs? Your customers already have the best hardware in their pockets.

## 3. The Table Top Solution (Value Proposition)
These sections mirror the pain points, providing the direct Table Top feature that solves them.

**Card 1 (The Tech): One QR Code. Live Updates.**
> No app to install. A gorgeous, premium digital menu that perfectly matches your brand and updates instantly from your dashboard.

**Card 2 (The Monetization): Turn Menus Into Billboards**
> The only platform that lets you sell hyper-local ad space inside your menu. Let the jeweler next door sponsor your appetizers. Your menu now prints money.

**Card 3 (The Experience): The 'Boredom Killer'**
> Customers hate waiting for food. Built-in retro arcade games keep them entertained, drastically reducing complaints and bad reviews.

## 4. Brand Voice Guidelines
* **Do NOT:** Use generic "SaaS-speak" (e.g., "Synergize your restaurant flow").
* **DO:** Use hospitality-first language (e.g., "A digital menu that looks as good as your food tastes").
* **Do NOT:** Shy away from calling out competitors (PDFs, Zomato, clunky ERPs).
* **DO:** Focus on margins, control, and aesthetic elegance.
